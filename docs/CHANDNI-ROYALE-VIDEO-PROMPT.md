# Chandni Royale — opening-film production brief

Use the supplied closed-gates image as FIRST FRAME and moonlit-balcony image as LAST FRAME. Both are original generated artwork for this Paigaam template. The website will add editable names/date in HTML after the film, so do not generate any lettering into the film.

## Delivery

- One full-screen vertical 9:16 film, ideally 1080 × 1920, 8–10 seconds, 24 or 30 fps.
- MP4, H.264, yuv420p; web-optimized fast-start. No captions, logo, phone frame, hands, filming-in-a-park background, or interface.
- If your generator supports only 8 seconds, use the 8-second timing below; do not speed up an existing longer film to fit it.
- Prefer no music. A very soft ribbon/gate movement sound is optional. The website will not autoplay music and accepts a separate optional customer-uploaded song.
- Preserve the final composition for at least the last one second. This is crucial for a seamless handoff to the invitation page.

## Copy-paste video prompt

A single continuous cinematic camera move through the royal palace wedding entrance shown in the supplied FIRST FRAME, ending in the exact composition and lighting of the supplied LAST FRAME. Vertical 9:16, ultra-detailed realistic materials and architectural perspective, restrained romantic luxury. Begin centered in front of the closed ornate champagne-gold filigree double gates, under the ivory rose-and-hydrangea floral crown. An ivory silk ribbon is tied into an elegant bow across the center. Antique-gold stone columns and warm candle sconces stand to each side, with dark oxblood velvet and midnight navy beyond.

0–1.5 seconds: hold the same symmetrical gate composition briefly, then the ivory silk bow softly loosens and unties by itself, with believable fabric weight and natural trailing ribbon movement. No hands or people appear. No exploding ribbon, no magic sparks.

1.5–4 seconds: the two heavy filigree gate leaves swing slowly INWARD on their outside hinges, preserving the gate design and precise rigid metal geometry. The camera begins a smooth, gentle forward dolly through the opening. Keep motion calm and stately. Reveal the velvet-draped palace balcony beyond, with warm candlelight on carved champagne stone.

4–7 seconds: continue the one unbroken forward dolly into the balcony, arriving at a perfectly centered tall round-topped arch with dark burgundy velvet curtains held to each side. Through the arch is a deep midnight-navy sky with a realistic large full moon high in the opening, a distant illuminated fairytale palace low on the horizon across a still lake, and a carved stone balustrade below. A few ivory roses and candle sconces sit at the sides. The moon and distant palace stay physically stable, no morphing. The lower stone floor remains visible. Preserve clear dark negative space through the middle for names to be added later in HTML.

7–8 seconds: ease the camera to a complete stop and HOLD the supplied LAST FRAME composition. No fade to black. No scene cut. The ending must match the supplied balcony image: the same arch, full moon position, drawn burgundy drapes, gold stone details, distant palace and marble floor. Do not add text or people.

## Negative prompt

Text, lettering, names, dates, captions, logos, watermarks, phone mockup, browser UI, human hands, people, faces, outdoor camera operator, park background, Indian temple, marigolds, banana leaves, futuristic portal, glitter storm, floating particles, cartoon style, plastic CGI, fisheye lens, warped filigree, bending pillars, melting metal, changing architecture, extra doors, flickering candles across frames, camera shake, rapid zoom, jump cuts, crossfades, fade-out, changing moon phase, moving moon, daytime ending, flat white ending.

## If your tool does not support both first and last frames

Use the closed-gates image as the first-frame reference, and add the balcony image as a visual reference with high influence. Repeat the entire final-frame composition in the prompt. Send the resulting film back even if it is not perfect; the still ending can be used for a brief visually matched transition if needed. Do not bake the couple names into the video.
